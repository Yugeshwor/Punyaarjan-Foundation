The Concept of Punyaarjan

We often think of human beings as fundamentally self-interested. Much of economics has traditionally been built around this idea: people make choices to improve their own well-being, seeking security, comfort, pleasure or satisfaction.

But look around, and another side of human nature is impossible to ignore. People give their time to help a neighbour. They donate to strangers, teach without pay, care for family members, volunteer for causes they believe in, and use their skills to solve problems that matter to them.

Why do we do these things?

One answer is that giving feels good.

In 1989, economist James Andreoni introduced an influential theory of “warm-glow” giving. He proposed that people can care both about the good their contributions do for others and about the satisfaction they derive from giving itself. The feeling of knowing I helped can be a reward in its own right.

In 2006, neuroscience research provided early evidence that charitable giving activates brain regions involved in reward. Subsequent research has linked giving to greater happiness and a stronger sense of purpose. Together, these findings offer a scientific basis for the idea that helping others can enrich our own lives, too.

This does not mean that people give simply to feel good. Generosity can arise from compassion, duty, love, solidarity, personal values or a genuine concern for another person’s well-being. Often, these motivations coexist.

Long before economists and neuroscientists studied the rewards of generosity, cultures, religions and philosophies around the world had recognised that doing good carries a value of its own. The Sanskrit word punya captures one expression of this idea: there is a moral or spiritual merit associated with good deeds.

Whether understood as warm glow, punya, or through the emerging science of generosity, these perspectives converge on the idea that when we make someone else’s life better, we enrich our own in the process. Not necessarily through material gain, but through a deeper sense of meaning, fulfilment, connection and the knowledge that our actions have made a difference.

This is the idea behind Punyaarjan.

Punyaarjan literally means “earning punya.” We believe that when we put what we have for the benefit of others, we create value on both sides. Our time, knowledge, skills, resources, relationships and influence can help others overcome challenges while giving us a deeper sense of purpose.

What we’re working on

We draw on a principle from the effective altruism community: to do the most good, focus on problems that are important, neglected, and solvable.

Healthcare in developing regions like Nepal is one such challenge. Despite the dedication of many healthcare workers and community initiatives, gaps in access to medical resources and expertise persist. Innovative technology offers new ways to address these gaps, but lasting progress depends on local commitment and the capacity to sustain solutions over time.

We believe the willingness to help is already there. But the connection between people who care, those who have the knowledge and resources to contribute, and the healthcare problems that need attention is what is missing.

We work to build these connections. We work to adapt technological innovation, strengthen community initiatives, and improve health education and awareness. We turn scattered goodwill into focused action in healthcare.

Whether you are an expert with knowledge to share, a young person eager to learn and contribute to our cause area, a professional looking to apply your skills beyond your job, or someone who cares about making things better, we invite you to join Punyaarjan.

Chronic Wound Healing

The problem

  • Chronic non-healing wounds, driven by rising global rates of diabetes, vascular diseases, and neglected infectious lesions like leprosy, affect millions worldwide and, without early intervention, frequently lead to severe tissue loss and avoidable lower-limb amputations.
  • Patients suffering from long-term open wounds endure continuous pain, restricted physical mobility, higher risks of recurrent severe infections, and profound social isolation and depression.
  • Treatment of non-healing wounds places an immense financial burden on vulnerable families through direct out-of-pocket medical expenses, while severe disability and lost productivity drain household incomes and strain overburdened healthcare systems.

A Simple, Safe, and Cost-Effective Solution

Natural guided regeneration using L-PRF (Leukocyte Platelet Rich Fibrin) is an autologous treatment approach, suitable for treating chronic wounds such as diabetic foot ulcers, venous ulcers, traumatic ulcers, leprosy foot ulcers, small burns and many more.

Diabetic ulcers are another silent crisis

1 in 5 people have diabetes in Nepal but nearly half remain undiagnosed. Diabetic foot ulcers are emerging as a major public health challenge. Without awareness, early screening, and access to care, preventable wounds will continue to drive avoidable amputations, disability, and socioeconomic hardship.

Help to Smile

The problem

  • Nepal faces a severe oral disease burden driven by high rates of dental caries, periodontal disease, and poor access to care.
  • Untreated oral diseases lead to severe pain, costly advanced treatments, lost work productivity, and significant absence from school among children.
  • Poor oral hygiene and gum diseases are also linked to an increased risk of major non-communicable diseases such as diabetes and cardiovascular diseases.

Most oral diseases are preventable and can be treated in their early stages.

— World Health Organization

We educate children, teachers and parents.

Chetu Kids Club is our innovative, child-centered initiative to make oral fitness fun by empowering children, parents and teachers to build healthy habits together through interactive, prevention-focused learning.

Learn more

Some children need specialized care

  • Approximately 1 in every 2000 live births in Nepal is a case of cleft lip or cleft lip and palate.
  • To grow up with good oral health, these children require specialized orthodontic care, preventive dental treatments, and guidance on oral hygiene to allow for proper tooth development and avoid severe decay and gum disease.

We are expanding access to oral care for children with cleft lip or palate.

Through our partnership with the Muskan Clinic at Kirtipur Hospital, we provide children and their families with the specialized care and guidance they need to maintain good oral hygiene.

Knowledge Philanthropy

In an age when knowledge is abundant and cheap, the ability to streamline that knowledge to solve problems remains invaluable.

We foster knowledge philanthropy by bringing together professionals, researchers and communities to share their expertise, experiences, and strengthen local capabilities to solve pressing healthcare problems in Nepal and the wider Global South.

Past events

March 2016Nepal

L-PRF Natural Guided Regeneration, Nepal Knowledge Transfer Series

In March 2016, Punyaarjan Foundation invited Prof. Dr. Nelson R. Pinto to Nepal to introduce Natural-Guided Regeneration Therapy (N-GRT) using leukocyte- and platelet-rich fibrin (L-PRF), inspired by his work treating chronic foot ulcers. During the technology transfer visit, Dr. Pinto trained medical and dental professionals across Nepal and inaugurated the first L-PRF wound-healing clinic at Kirtipur Hospital, laying the foundation for affordable chronic wound care in resource-constrained settings.

16–18 March 2018Nepal

Asian Congress of Tissue Regeneration in Medicine & Oral Implantology

Under the Punyaarjan Chronic Wound Healing Project, and in collaboration with the Asian College of Oral Implantology & Tissue Regeneration, Public Health Concern Trust Nepal, National Dental Hospital, and Nepal Oral Implantology Association, the Punyaarjan Foundation organized a 3 day-long congress on Tissue Regeneration in Medicine and Oral Implantology in Nepal, with speakers from 11 countries.

The Congress speakers included Prof. Dr. Nelson R. Pinto (Chile), Prof. Dott. Paolo G. Morselli (Italy), Dr. Robert Horowitz (USA), Dr. Myat Nyan (Myanmar), Dr. Chakra Raj Pandey (Nepal), Dr. Nicea Baptista Pardo (Brazil), Dr. Jinhwan Kim (Korea), Dr. Rajapas Panichuttra (Thailand), Dr. Mostaque Sattar (Bangladesh), Dr. Ashis Kakar (India), Dr. Lanka Mahesh (India) and Dr. Sandeep Singh (India). These renowned speakers, each an expert in the application of tissue regeneration in their field, voluntarily participated as Punyaarjan Knowledge Philanthropists, selflessly sharing their knowledge for the benefit of the profession and humanity.

The conference moderators included Prof. Dr. Keshav D Joshi, Prof. Dr. Shaili Pradhan, Prof. Dr. Binod Acharya, Prof. Dr. Sarita Joshi, Prof. Dr. Suraj B Mathema and Prof. Dr. Surendra M Shrestha.

Two workshops were also conducted during the three days. The pre-congress workshop on Advanced Techniques in Tissue Regeneration & Bone Management was moderated by Prof. Dr. Myat Nyan (Myanmar), with speakers Dr. Robert Horowitz (USA) and Prof. Dr. Nelson R Pinto (Chile). Likewise, the post-congress workshop on Implant Occlusion & Prosthetic Design was moderated by Dr. Jinhwan Kim (Korea), with speakers Dr. Rajapas Panichuttra (Thailand), Dr. Vikas Aggarwal (India), and Dr. Sushil Koirala (Nepal).

7–9 September 2018Belgium

Second European Meeting on Enhanced Natural Healing in Dentistry

The Catholic University of Leuven invited the Punyaarjan Foundation to the 2nd European Meeting on Enhanced Natural Healing in Dentistry in September 2018. This allowed the Foundation to showcase the achievements of its Chronic Wound Healing Project, especially in treating chronic extraoral wounds with L-PRF, like Leprosy plantar ulcers. This event marked a milestone in advocating for the widespread adoption of this affordable wound-healing technology. Special thanks to the Congress Committee, specifically Prof. Dr. Marc Quirynen and Prof. Dr. Nelson Pinto for organizing the successful event, attended by over 450 participants from 40 countries.
